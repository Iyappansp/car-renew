Coverage education

Understand your car insurance before you choose.

Every plan trades off cost against protection differently. Here's what each one actually means.

Third-Party

Third-party insurance

The minimum legally required cover in most regions. It protects other people and their property if you're at fault — not your own vehicle.

What it generally covers
  • Injury to third parties
  • Damage to third-party property
  • Legal liability cover
Who needs it
  • Every registered vehicle owner, at minimum
  • Older vehicles with lower resale value
What it does not cover
  • Damage to your own vehicle
  • Theft of your own vehicle
Comprehensive

Comprehensive insurance

Combines third-party liability with cover for damage to your own vehicle — the most commonly chosen balance of cost and protection.

Third-party liability
  • Same protection as third-party plans
Own-damage protection
  • Accidental damage to your car
  • Fire and theft, in most policies
Important exclusions
  • Depreciation deducted on parts
  • Wear-and-tear related issues

Add-ons

Extend your comprehensive plan.

Zero depreciation

Claims pay out without a depreciation deduction on parts. Helps most with newer vehicles.

Roadside assistance

Support for breakdowns, towing or minor on-road issues. Useful for longer commutes.

Engine protection

Covers engine damage from water ingress or oil leakage. Worth considering in flood-prone areas.

Consumables

Covers items like nuts, bolts, fluids and lubricants that typically aren't included by default.

Personal accident cover

Provides cover for the owner-driver in case of accidental injury.
